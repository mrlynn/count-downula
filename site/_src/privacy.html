Privacy policy

Effective October 9, 2026

Count Downcula doesn't collect your data. Your countdowns live on your devices and in your own private iCloud, where I can't see them. The one exception is a countdown you choose to share as a live link: that one is copied to Count Downcula's server so anyone with the link can see it, until you stop sharing it. The apps also send anonymous usage counts, like how many countdowns get made, which you can turn off. This website doesn't track you either.

This policy covers the Count Downcula apps for macOS, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Apple TV, the Mac screensaver, the website at www.countdowncula.com, and the live link pages at go.countdowncula.com, including the countdown editor there. Both are made by Michael Lynn ("I", "me"). If anything here is unclear, email merlynn@gmail.com.

The apps

Count Downcula has no sign-up, no advertising, no crash reporting and no third-party analytics. Apart from live links (see below) and the anonymous usage counts described here, it doesn't talk to any server of mine, and I never receive anything you put into it.

  • Your countdowns. Titles, notes, dates, timers and photos are saved on each device where you use Count Downcula. On a Mac they live in ~/Library/Application Support/Countdownula/. Photos are resized, and a small thumbnail is made for the menu bar, widgets and the watch.
  • iCloud sync. From version 1.1, if you're signed in to iCloud, your countdowns and photos sync between your Mac, iPhone and Apple Watch through your private iCloud database. Apple runs this service under Apple's privacy policy. Data in your private database belongs to your Apple Account, and I can't access it. If iCloud is off or unavailable, Count Downcula keeps working and saves everything on the device only.
  • iPhone. The iPhone app stores your countdowns on the phone and syncs them through the same private iCloud database. Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets and Live Activities read a copy of your countdowns and photos that stays on the phone. Photos you add are chosen with the system photo picker, so the app only sees the photos you pick, never the rest of your library.
  • Location. The iPhone app asks for your location only when you add a sunrise or sunset countdown, and only once for that countdown. It asks iOS for an approximate position, not your precise one, and rounds it to about 11 km (a tenth of a degree). That rounded position is saved with the countdown so it can work out the next sunrise or sunset, and it syncs with the countdown through your private iCloud. It's never sent to me, and it isn't included if you share that countdown as a live link. Deleting the countdown deletes it. Full moon countdowns don't use your location.
  • Usage counts. With Share Analytics on (it's on unless you turn it off), the iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple TV apps and the App Clip send Count Downcula's server a short note when certain things happen:
    • the app is used that day
    • a countdown is made (and how, such as typed, from a screenshot, from Calendar or added on Apple TV), deleted (and whether before or after it finished), or reaches zero, or is kept counting up
    • a countdown is switched to a different way of counting, such as sleeps or weeks
    • a share sheet is opened, or a card or video is exported
    • a button under an alert, a widget button or a Control Center control is used
    • a countdown is shown full screen on a Mac or TV, and whether it was still showing at zero
    • the Unlimited offer is shown, or Unlimited or a Host Pass is bought
    • a countdown kept in the App Clip arrives in the app
    Each note says only what happened and when, the app version, the kind of device (iPhone, iPad, Mac or Apple TV), for some notes how it happened or which way of counting was used, and for shared countdowns the link's short code. It never includes titles, notes, dates, photos, names, contacts or calendar contents. To tell one install's notes apart, the app makes up a random ID that isn't connected to you, your device or your Apple Account, and you can reset it. The notes are kept for 13 months and then deleted. They're used only to see which features people use and to improve the app, never for advertising, and they're never sold or shared. The Apple Watch app and the screensaver don't send them.
  • Calendar and Contacts. If you add countdowns from Calendar, the iPhone or Mac app asks to read your calendars and shows the next 90 days of events on that device. If you add birthdays from Contacts on iPhone, it uses the system picker, so it only ever sees the people you pick, and only their name and birthday. Neither your events nor your contacts are sent anywhere. Only the countdowns you make from them sync, through your private iCloud like any other.
  • Time zones and ways of counting. A countdown can be set in another time zone, and counted in weeks, sleeps (from a bedtime you choose), workdays, weekends or percent. These are saved with the countdown and sync through your private iCloud like the rest of it.
  • Apple TV. The Apple TV app shows the countdowns in your private iCloud, the same as your other devices. The Top Shelf reads a copy of upcoming countdowns that stays on the Apple TV. Adding a Crypt countdown on the TV joins it the same way as on iPhone (see Live links).
  • Mac screensaver. The screensaver, which comes with the version of the Mac app downloaded from countdowncula.com or GitHub, shows a copy of your countdowns that the Mac app keeps in the screensaver's own folder on your Mac. Nothing it shows leaves the Mac.
  • Apple Watch. The watch app stores your countdowns on the watch and syncs them through the same private iCloud database. Watch face complications read a small summary of upcoming countdowns that stays on the watch.
  • Notifications. Each device asks for permission to alert you when a countdown finishes. Alerts are scheduled and delivered locally by macOS, iOS or watchOS.
  • Backups. If you use Time Machine or another backup tool, your countdowns are backed up along with the rest of your files, under that tool's own terms.

Live links

On iPhone you can share a countdown as a live link: a web page at go.countdowncula.com that ticks live and shows a preview when the link is pasted into a message. Nothing is shared until you tap Share Live Link and confirm. Only that countdown is shared; your other countdowns stay private.

  • What's sent. The countdown's title, description, date, type (countdown, timer or count-up), style, milestones, photo and way of counting (with the bedtime, for sleeps), plus its time zone (yours, unless you set one for it) so dates show the way you see them. People who join see the date in that time zone as well as their own. No name, email, Apple Account, location or device identifier is sent.
  • Who can see it. Anyone who has the link. Links use random addresses that can't be guessed or listed, but anyone you send one to can pass it on.
  • Where it's kept. On Count Downcula's server, hosted by Vercel, with the countdown stored in a MongoDB Atlas database run by MongoDB, Inc. See Vercel's privacy policy and MongoDB's privacy policy.
  • Making one on the web. You can also make a countdown at go.countdowncula.com/new without the app. It's stored the same way as a live link shared from the app. Instead of your iCloud Keychain, your browser keeps the private key that edits it, in its own storage on that device. If you ask for an edit link by email, your address is used once to send it (through Resend, see Reports) and isn't stored; the key travels in the part of the link after the #, which browsers don't send to the server.
  • Editing it. When you share a link, the server gives the app a private key, which is kept in your iCloud Keychain so your other devices can update the page. The server stores only a scrambled (hashed) copy of that key. Edits you make to the countdown update the page.
  • Joining someone's countdown. When you join a shared countdown, the server stores a scrambled (hashed) member key and the time you joined, so the owner can see how many people are counting down and your devices can take you back out. It stores no name or account. The countdown itself syncs to your devices through your private iCloud like any other. Leaving, or the owner stopping sharing, deletes your member record.
  • The sealed coffin. On a shared countdown, the owner and members can leave a note and a photo, with the name they choose to sign it with. Notes are stored on Count Downcula's server and photos in Vercel Blob storage. Anyone with the link can also leave one from the web page; their browser keeps a key the server hands it, and the server stores only a scrambled (hashed) copy. Nobody can see them until the countdown reaches zero, except the person who left them; then everyone in the shared countdown, and everyone who left something from the web, can. The owner can remove anything, you can remove your own, and stopping sharing deletes all of them.
  • Host Passes. If you buy a Host Pass for a shared countdown, the app sends Count Downcula's server the purchase as Apple signs it. The server checks Apple's signature and keeps the transaction number, when it was bought, and which countdown it hosts, so a pass can't be used twice. Apple handles the payment; I never see your card or Apple Account. A hosted countdown can have a custom link, and its coffin notes can carry several photos and a short video, kept the same way as other coffin photos.
  • Calendar feeds. Each shared countdown has a calendar feed people can subscribe to. To count subscriptions, the server notes a scrambled (salted and hashed) form of the address that fetched the feed and which calendar app it was, never the address itself, and forgets it 45 days after the last fetch.
  • Embeds. A countdown can be shown on another website. The server counts how often it's shown and notes the name of the site it's on (for example example.com), not who saw it.
  • Date pools. If a shared countdown has a date pool, anyone with the link can guess when it will happen, with the name they type. The server stores each guess, that name and a scrambled (hashed) key so the guesser can change it later; a browser keeps its key in its own storage. Everyone with the link can see the guesses. The owner can remove any guess, you can remove your own before the real date is set, and stopping sharing deletes all of them.
  • Reports. If someone reports a note or photo, I'm sent an email (through Resend, under Resend's privacy policy) with the name and note so I can review it and remove it if it breaks the rules. Nothing about the person who reported it is included.
  • Apple Wallet passes. If you add a shared countdown to Apple Wallet, Wallet registers the pass with Count Downcula's server using an identifier and push token that Apple generates for that purpose, so the pass can update when the countdown changes. They aren't linked to you, and removing the pass from Wallet deletes them.
  • Page views and activity. The server keeps a running count of how many times each page has been viewed. It also notes each view, share, unshare, join, leave, coffin note, pool guess, Wallet pass, calendar subscription and Host Pass. It notes when a countdown is opened full screen for a TV (present mode), and whether that page was still open at zero, and when the web editor is opened (and from where, such as a live page's Make your own button). Each note has the time, the link's short code, whether it came from a browser or the app (and which version), and for page views the name of the referring site (for example google.com, never the full address). It doesn't record who did it or their IP address. These notes are kept for 13 months and then deleted.
  • Abuse limits. To stop anyone flooding the server, it counts how often each connection creates, edits or removes links. Connections are told apart by a scrambled (salted and hashed) form of the IP address, never the address itself, and each count is deleted when its hour or day is over.
  • Stopping. Tap Stop Sharing Link, or delete the countdown on a device that can edit the link. The page and its copy on the server, including the photo, are deleted straight away. The link stops working for everyone.

I don't use shared countdowns for anything except showing them at their link. I don't look through them, sell them or use them for advertising. If someone reports a page as abusive or illegal, I may look at it and take it down.

The website

The website is a set of static pages. It uses no analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixels, no embedded social media and no cookies. Fonts and images are served from this site, not from third parties. The "try it" countdown runs entirely in your browser, and nothing you type into it is saved or sent.

The site is hosted by Vercel. Like any web host, Vercel processes basic request information, such as your IP address, browser type and the page requested, to deliver pages and protect against abuse. See Vercel's privacy policy for how they handle it. I don't use this information to identify or profile you.

The download link goes to GitHub, which has its own privacy statement.

When you email me

If you write to me for support, I'll receive your email address and whatever you include in the message. I use it only to reply and to fix what you reported. I don't add you to a mailing list, and I don't share or sell it. You can ask me to delete our correspondence at any time.

Children

Count Downcula doesn't knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13. Live links share only what the person sharing them chooses to put in a countdown.

Your rights

Apart from countdowns you've shared as live links and any emails you've sent me, there is nothing about you on my side to access, export or delete. Your countdowns are under your control:

  • Deleting a countdown in the app deletes it everywhere it syncs.
  • To stop sending usage counts, turn off Share Analytics: on iPhone and iPad in the Settings app under Apps, Count Downcula; on a Mac in the menu bar list's privacy menu (the hand icon); on Apple TV in the app's Settings tab. Turning it off deletes any counts waiting to be sent. Reset Analytics ID in the same place starts over with a new random ID.
  • To stop syncing, turn off iCloud for Count Downcula in your Apple Account's iCloud settings. Your countdowns stay on the device.
  • To remove a live link and its copy on the server, tap Stop Sharing Link, or for one made on the web, open its edit page and delete it. If you've lost every device and browser that can edit the link, email me the link and I'll delete it.
  • To remove the iCloud copy, delete Count Downcula's data under Manage Storage in your iCloud settings. To remove the copy on a Mac, delete the folder above. On iPhone, removing the app removes its copy.
